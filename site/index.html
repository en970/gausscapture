The question

Can signals measured on your phone during capture predict the quality of a reconstruction that has not been trained yet?

Every phone-capture app tells you a scan failed after it fails. Coverage grids and “move slower” prompts are qualitative; none of them emit a number.

The individual signals are well studied in isolation. Motion-blur compensation is worth +1.68 PSNR on real phone captures. Correcting auto-exposure drift is worth up to +5.3 dB. But they have never been composed and validated as a predictor, and no public dataset pairs capture-time telemetry with reconstruction outcomes.

Three findings

01

The capture side is the open problem

The reconstruction side is crowded — gsplat, Brush, LichtFeld, a dozen algorithmic variants. The capture side has two hobby-scale iOS apps and no science. That asymmetry is the opportunity.

02

Stationary-phone 4D is bounded by physics, not engineering

With zero camera parallax, the occluded side of a moving subject is not in the recording. Published results reach ~19–22 dB masked PSNR inside a ±30° cone. Past that, methods hallucinate — and every prior that fills the gap is unreleased, non-commercial, or needs 32 GB200s.

03

Almost none of this literature is usable under MIT

Every released monocular-dynamic method is encumbered through the Inria rasterizer, CoTracker3, or UniDepth — and no author has removed all three. Several repositories declare a permissive licence while shipping encumbered code.

The 4D reality

The long-term goal is a phone on a table reconstructing a moving subject. Here is what that actually buys you.

So what ships

A bounded bullet-time card: static background plus dynamic foreground, 30–60 second clips, with the viewer hard-clamped to the trained ±25° cone and the cone labelled in the interface. Users should not be able to orbit into hallucinated geometry and conclude the tool is broken.

The tractable route to genuine novel views is a sparse two-to-four-phone mode — the same capture app, the same container, but a solvable sparse-multiview problem instead of an unwinnable hallucination problem.

The permissive stack

GaussCapture users must be able to ship what they make. That rules out most of the ecosystem.

Roadmap

  1. v0 · now

    Foundation

    Public repository, REUSE-compliant SPDX, DCO, Zenodo DOI. Extract a pip-installable core so the FastAPI and React layers become thin clients.

    Gate: reuse lint clean; pip install gausscapture works on three platforms.

  2. v1

    Evaluation harness

    Not UI polish — a headless CLI that turns 180 capture packs into 180 reconstructions unattended, with pinned dependencies. gsplat MCMC wired correctly; Spark replaces the deprecated viewers.

    Gate: Mip-NeRF 360 within 0.3 dB of gsplat's published 29.15.

  3. v2 · the paper

    Capture intelligence

    On-device coach, native capture app, 24 scenes across three deliberate quality tiers, and the correlation study.

    Pre-registered: VoL-p10 reaches |ρ| ≥ 0.40 and registration-failure AUC ≥ 0.80 — or the paper pivots and we publish the negative result.

  4. v3

    Dynamic, bounded

    Background/foreground decomposition, motion scaffold, and 16 dynamic scenes captured with witness phones at ±25° and ±45° supplying genuine held-out ground truth — the benchmark that currently does not exist.

    Gate: DyCheck masked mPSNR ≥ 18.5; dataset DOI resolves.

No CUDA on your machine? There is no Metal training path worth using — Brush takes about two hours for a 30k-step scene, while renting an RTX 4090 costs $0.34/hour, making the same run cost $0.17. Rent.

Contributing

The most useful contribution right now is a capture that failed. If you filmed a scene and the reconstruction came out badly, attach the quality report and describe how you filmed it — lighting, walking speed, whether exposure was locked, how many orbits.

That is precisely the data this project is trying to turn into a predictor.
