GaussCapture for Android

A capture app for 3D Gaussian splatting. It records video, the camera's own per-frame metadata and the motion sensors on one shared clock, and writes a folder your computer can reconstruct from. Android 9 or newer, and a stand to put the phone on. It is not on Google Play; you install the APK yourself.

Installing it outside the store

Android will not install an APK that did not come from a store until you tell it that the app you downloaded it with is allowed to do that. This is a prompt, not an error, and if nobody warns you about it the install looks broken.

  1. Download the file on the phone, or copy it across and open it from the Files app.
  2. Tap it. Android says something like “For your security, your phone is not allowed to install unknown apps from this source.” Choose Settings.
  3. Turn on Allow from this source — you are granting it to Chrome or Files, not to the world — then go back and tap the file again.
  4. Play Protect may add “Unsafe app blocked” or offer to scan it. That warning is about provenance, not about anything it found: Google has not seen this app before. Choose Install anyway or More details → Install anyway.
  5. Turn the permission back off afterwards if you like. It is per-app and it sticks.

Updating. Android refuses to replace an installed app with a build signed by a different key. If an install fails with a signature error, uninstall the old copy first. Captures that reached Documents/GaussCapture survive an uninstall; captures still in the app's private folder — which is where they land if you declined the storage permission — do not. Copy them off first.

What it records

One take produces a folder — a capture pack — not just a video. The pack is the point: a video on its own throws away the timing and the motion that make it reconstructable.

In the packWhat it is for
The videoH.264 in an MP4 — 1080p where the camera offers it, with the frame rate pinned at 30.
Per-frame camera metadataExposure, ISO, focus distance, lens intrinsics and the sensor timestamp of every single frame.
Sensor streamsGyroscope and accelerometer, calibrated and uncalibrated, plus both rotation vectors — logged on a thread of their own at the highest rate the phone allows.
A manifestWhich protocol was shot, where each phase started and ended, whether the phone was measurably at rest, how far the sweep actually went, and every problem the app noticed while recording.

Frame timestamps and sensor timestamps are written from one hardware clock, which is what lets a frame be paired with the accelerometer reading taken at that instant. That pairing is how the world's up direction is recovered — measured at 0.998 agreement across a capture, against heuristics that were wrong by as much as 41 degrees.

Packs are written to Documents/GaussCapture so a computer can see them over USB. Granting the storage permission is what puts them there; refuse it and the app keeps working, but the folder is only reachable over adb.

Six capture protocols ship: three ordinary sweeps at different paces, two that are expected to fail — rotation on the spot, and a moving subject filmed from a stationary phone — so you can see what failure looks like, and the four-phase protocol below.

The four-phase capture

F · Bullet time is one recording with a script inside it. It is the only one of the six that can reconstruct a moving subject, and it is the reason the phone needs something to sit on.

2 sperch

Hold still

Phone in its support, your face framed, hands off. This is the pose the finished result is shot from, held long enough for exposure and white balance to settle before anything is locked.

12 sarc

Sweep around

Lift the phone and sweep it about 60° around your own face — and you hold still while you do it. This is the only phase that contains geometry: while you are still, your head is part of a rigid scene and triangulates like the wall behind you. A camera that never moves cannot produce this, at any exposure, ever.

~3 sreseat

Phone back

Put it back where it was and let go. This phase ends on a measurement, never on a clock: the gyroscope and accelerometer together decide when the phone is actually at rest, and the countdown restarts the moment you touch it again.

3 shold

Now move

Talk, smile, turn your head. The phone stays put. The recording stops itself — reaching over to press stop would move the camera, and the frames it would corrupt are the ones closest to the motion the take exists to capture.

One file rather than four, because the phase that carries the geometry and the phase that carries the motion have to share an exposure, a white balance and a clock — and because stopping the recorder to start it again moves the phone.

You need a support. A stand, a tripod, a case propped against a mug — the requirement is only that the phone does not move at all during the last two phases and that you can put it back roughly where it was. Every frame of the hold is given the same camera pose downstream; if the phone is still settling, that assumption is false and nothing later would ever say so.

What you get, and what you do not

The result is a bounded card, not a free orbit. You can move the viewpoint inside a measured cone around the camera axis, and you can scrub time. You cannot walk around the subject.

Static scenes have no such limit. Walk a slow circle around an object with one of the ordinary sweeps and you get a splat you can view from anywhere; the cone is the price of the camera standing still, and nothing else.

Requirements

OS
Android 9.0 (API 28) or newer. There is no iOS build.
Hardware
A gyroscope and an accelerometer, both required — the app will not install on a phone without them. A front camera for the four-phase protocol, which films you.
Support
Something to stand the phone on, for the fixed-camera phases. Handheld will not do: the whole method rests on the camera being measurably at rest.
Storage
About 46 MB to download — one APK carrying all three architectures — plus room for the captures themselves, which are 1080p video and run to a few hundred megabytes a minute.
Permissions
Camera, asked at first use. All-files access, so packs land in Documents/GaussCapture where your computer can see them; decline it and the app falls back to private storage. High-rate sensor access, which Android requires for anything above 200 Hz — without it the gyroscope is silently capped and the pack is worth less.
Network
None. The release build declares no INTERNET permission at all — Android will not let it open a socket even if some future dependency tried to. Nothing is uploaded, there is no analytics, and everything it produces stays on the phone until you copy it off.

Then what

Copy the pack off the phone and hand it to the command-line tool, which does the quality analysis, the pose solve and the export locally. It is not on PyPI yet, so it comes from the repository:

brew install ffmpeg colmap
git clone https://github.com/en970/gausscapture
cd gausscapture && ./install_macos.sh

gausscapture doctor              # what is missing on this machine
gausscapture import PACK.capturepack   # read the pack into a project
gausscapture telemetry PROJECT         # was this capture any good?

Training itself needs a CUDA GPU, and there is no Metal path worth using; everything either side of it — import, quality analysis, frame extraction, pose, masking, export and the viewer — runs on an ordinary laptop.

The 4D stages are still landing. The app records the four-phase capture today and the pack is complete and readable. On the desktop side the bounded bullet-time path — prep4d, train4d, export4d, viewer4d — is present but not yet end to end; the static pipeline is the one that works today. The README and the roadmap say which is which, and are the thing to trust over this page if they disagree.
